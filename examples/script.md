## Narration (~380 words)

### 1. Hook (0:00–0:15)
What if I told you that you're not procrastinating because you're lazy?
In fact, you might be procrastinating because your brain is trying to protect you.
Meet Leo.
Leo has a big dream. He wants to start his own business. He's got ideas, plans, and even a notebook.
There's just one problem.
He never actually starts.

### 2. The endless tomorrow (0:15–0:42)
Every morning, Leo tells himself, "Today is the day."
But then he checks his phone.
Watches one video.
Makes another coffee.
And suddenly, it's midnight.
"Tomorrow," he says.
Tomorrow becomes next Monday. Next Monday becomes next month.
And every night, Leo feels terrible.
He calls himself undisciplined. Weak. Lazy.
But here's where things get interesting.

### 3. The twist (0:42–1:10)
One evening, Leo asks himself a different question.
"What if I'm not avoiding the work? What if I'm avoiding how the work makes me feel?"
And suddenly, everything makes sense.
Starting his business means facing uncertainty.
What if he fails?
What if people judge him?
What if he's not good enough?
His brain sees these uncomfortable emotions as threats.
So it offers an escape.
Scroll. Snack. Watch something.
Instant relief.
That's the twist: procrastination isn't always a time-management problem. It's often an emotion-management problem.
Leo isn't avoiding effort.
He's avoiding discomfort.

### 4. The four changes (1:10–2:12)
But once Leo understands this, he can finally change.
And he starts with four simple tricks.
First: The two-minute start.
Instead of promising to work for three hours, Leo promises just two minutes.
Open the laptop. Write one sentence.
That's it.
Because starting is often harder than continuing.
Second: Shrink the task.
"Build a business" feels terrifying.
"Write down three ideas" feels manageable.
Big goals create pressure. Tiny actions create momentum.
Third: Remove friction.
Leo puts his phone in another room, opens his notebook before bed, and prepares his workspace.
He makes distractions harder and good habits easier.
Fourth: Practice self-compassion.
When Leo misses a day, he stops insulting himself.
He doesn't say, "I'm hopeless."
He says, "Okay. Yesterday was difficult. Let's try again today."
Because shame doesn't create lasting motivation.
It often creates more avoidance.

### 5. Ending (2:12–2:30)
Weeks later, Leo hasn't become a completely different person.
He still has difficult days.
But now, he knows something powerful.
He doesn't need to feel ready to begin.
He just needs to make beginning easier.
So remember:
You're not lazy just because you're struggling to start.
Sometimes, you just need to stop fighting yourself and take one tiny step forward.
And if this video helped you understand yourself a little better, subscribe for more psychology and motivation explained simply.
Your future doesn't need a perfect you. It needs a starting you.
