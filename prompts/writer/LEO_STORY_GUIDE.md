# How to write a Leo story (the owner's rule, 21.11.2026: «Simpla, frumoasa, adevărată»)

You write ONE short, funny, everyday story. It is read aloud in a short vertical video, so it must be understood the first
time, by someone who has never heard of the characters.

Leo: an ordinary young man who lives alone with his cat. Kind, a little stubborn, he talks to the cat as if it were a person.
Biscuit: his orange tabby cat. A cat, nothing more.

## The seven signs of a good story
1. An everyday situation from any home: cooking, a work call, a door, the sofa, the laundry. Nothing invented, nothing to explain.
2. The cat acts like a cat: it stares, meows, sits on things, walks away, sleeps. It has no plan, puts up no signs, never speaks.
3. One thread. Each thing happens because of the one before it.
4. Leo talks to the cat, and the humour is in how he answers it.
5. The psychology is named simply by someone in the story (a friend, a vet, a neighbour, something Leo reads), and Leo tries
   to apply it.
6. The turn at the end is logical: it follows from what the cat and Leo already did.
7. The lesson is said at the end, in one or two sentences a viewer could repeat to a friend.

## Never
A lever, a machine, a trap, food falling from the ceiling, a cat with a plan, anything that could not happen in an ordinary
home. More than one gag chasing the same idea. A line that sounds clever and cannot be understood at once.

## The model (written in the owner's own ChatGPT; this is the level and the shape)
Leo's cat had an annoying habit. Every evening Biscuit sat at the bedroom door and meowed. Leo opened it. Biscuit walked in,
looked around, and wanted out. Leo opened it again. Two minutes later Biscuit wanted in.
One evening, after six trips to the door, Leo snapped: "Do you need a door or a doorman?!"
The next day he told a friend.
"You know what the problem is?" said the friend. "You taught him that if he meows, you open."
"So it's my fault?"
"Exactly. It's called positive reinforcement. Behaviour that gets a reward gets repeated."
That evening Biscuit meowed at the door. Leo ignored him. Biscuit meowed louder. Leo put his headphones on. Biscuit scratched
the door, softly.
"I won't give in," Leo whispered. "I know psychology now."
Ten minutes later it went quiet. Leo smiled. "It works!" He got up for a glass of water. When he opened the door, Biscuit was
sitting there, calm. He walked straight into the bedroom.
Leo stood still. "Wait. Did I just wait for you to be quiet so I could open the door?"
Biscuit stretched out on the pillow.
"I think you ran the experiment on me."
LESSON: Behaviour that is followed by something pleasant gets repeated. Sometimes we keep alive exactly the behaviour that
annoys us, because our reaction is the reward.

## What to return
The story in plain prose with dialogue, in English, in the length the request gives. Start each spoken line with the
speaker's name and a colon (Leo:, Friend:). Biscuit has no lines. End with "LESSON:" and one or two sentences. Nothing else:
no scene numbers, no camera, no picture descriptions.
